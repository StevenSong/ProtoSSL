# MIMIC and ZZU → PTB-XL prototype cross-mapping: prespecification

Written before any MIMIC × PTB-XL or ZZU × PTB-XL association was computed. The commit
that adds this file, `make_expected_pairs_mimic_zzu.py` and `expected_pairs_mimic_zzu.csv`
is the record of what was fixed in advance.

This is the EchoNext analysis (`PRESPEC.md`, 754f94a) applied to the other two datasets
with non-classical labels. The rules are unchanged so the three datasets can be compared.
Two things learned from EchoNext are added as prespecified steps rather than post hoc:
the random-positives null (step 6) and positive controls (step 7).

## Question

Do any MIMIC labels (ED visit diagnoses, treatments and outcomes) or ZZU labels (pediatric
diagnoses) rely on the same pretrained prototypes as a PTB-XL statement that we would
**not** expect to be related? And is any such surprise more than a weak prototype
pigeonholed into PTB-XL's assignment of 14 prototypes to each of its 71 statements?

## Inputs

- `association_matrix.npy` and `assignment_metadata.csv` from
  `protossl-heedb-pila/learn-prototype-assignments/latest`, `runs-{mimic,zzu,ptbxl}`,
  seeds 42, 67, 70, 73, 99, under `$PROTOSSL_OUTPUTS/ecg`. Shapes: MIMIC 1000 × 46,
  ZZU 1000 × 4, PTB-XL 1000 × 71.
- For step 6, activations of PTB-XL's 994 projected prototypes:
  - PTB-XL train, from PTB-XL's own `compute-embeddings`;
  - MIMIC train and test, from job 8 (`protossl-heedb-pila-from-ptbxl`, run `recur`,
    seed 42), already published;
  - ZZU train and test, from job 8 on ZZU. **Not run yet.** Step 6 for ZZU is done once
    that job is published, with no other change.
- ProtoSSL reads MIMIC at 100 Hz, so the stale 500 Hz MIMIC caches (aVL/aVF swap) don't
  affect any input here.

## What was already known when this was written

- Every EchoNext × PTB-XL result in `echonext-ptbxl-crossmap.ipynb` and the EchoNext
  patient-level screen (8edf734). In particular:
  - |ρ| ≥ 0.5 is inside the noise: random positives reach |ρ| ≈ 0.6 at every n.
  - The "same sign" specificity rule is too lenient to remove generic statements.
  - The ILP map produces convincing false pairings on rare PTB-XL statements.
  - The specificity null is underpowered: textbook EchoNext pairs reach only q 0.14–0.21.
- The EchoNext × MIMIC overlap screen: LVSD (`LVEF Lo` ~ HFrEF) and AS (`AS` ~
  nonrheumatic aortic valve disorders) rank #1 and #2 of 552 pairs; `LVWT Hi` ~ ESRD is
  third. The shared prototypes have PTB-XL readings MI/aneurysm/ILBBB (LVSD) and
  LVH/LPR/CRBBB/ILBBB (AS). ZZU and the mortality labels overlap with EchoNext at about 0.
- On MIMIC val/test, patient-level adjustment of HFrEF and AV disorders for imputed PTB-XL
  statements and machine reads (`proto-recur-nonclassical.ipynb`). No MIMIC label other
  than those two has been compared with any PTB-XL statement, at the prototype or the
  patient level. No ZZU label has.
- The positive control from classical datasets (CODE-15%/CinC): correct matches at
  ρ 0.55–0.92, plus the false-surprise types AF→DIG (treatment) and RBBB→RVH
  (co-labeling within PTB-XL).

## Expected pairs

`expected_pairs_mimic_zzu.csv` is generated by `make_expected_pairs_mimic_zzu.py` from
named statement groups (MI, injury, ST-T, LVH, RV, LV conduction, AV block, atrial, ventricular
ectopy). The levels are as in `PRESPEC.md`: **expected** (established correlate) and
**plausible** (comorbidity, treatment, devices, secondary physiology). A label, statement
and sign not listed is **unexpected**; one listed only as plausible is **not expected**.

- NORM and SR are expected negative and SARRH plausibly negative for every label, except
  MIMIC `Non-cardiac diagnosis`.
  - That label is positive for about 99% of ECGs. Its negatives are visits with only
    cardiac diagnoses.
  - So for it, every cardiac reading is plausibly *negative*, and NORM/SR/SARRH are
    plausibly positive.
- The MIMIC labels are adult ED visits. Every abnormal statement except WPW is plausibly
  positive for every MIMIC label, through comorbid cardiovascular disease. WPW is plausible
  only where there's a path (AF, flutter, arrest, cardiomyopathy, VT).
  - So for MIMIC, a positive *unexpected* pairing can only involve WPW. Otherwise the
    unexpected tier is a sign reversal: an abnormal statement that goes *with* a normal
    outcome. Discovery in MIMIC therefore rests mostly on the **not expected** tier together
    with specificity.
- Mechanical mirrors are listed as plausible negatives: SBRAD where STACH is listed, and
  HVOLT/VCLVH where LVOLT is listed. EchoNext's PEff ~ −HVOLT was this kind of omission.
- MIMIC labels matching an EchoNext label inherit that label's rows from
  `expected_pairs.csv` (754f94a), unchanged:
  - HFrEF, CHF NOS and fluid overload take `LVEF Lo`;
  - HFpEF takes `LVWT Hi`;
  - aortic valve disorders take `AS` + `AR`;
  - heart valve replaced takes `AS` + `AR` + `MR`;
  - chronic pulmonary heart disease takes `PASP Hi` + `RVD`;
  - cardiomyopathies (MIMIC and ZZU) take `LVEF Lo` + `LVWT Hi`.
- ZZU is pediatric. Children's normal variants (RV dominance, juvenile T inversion in
  V1–V3, faster rates, sinus arrhythmia) occur in the ZZU negatives as well, so they are not
  listed as expected for any ZZU label.
- The list is provisional: drafted by an AI assistant from general clinical knowledge,
  pending cardiologist review. Additions after the results are seen are reported as post
  hoc and don't change the prespecified classification.

## Analysis

Each dataset is analyzed on its own: MIMIC has 46 × 71 = 3266 pairs, ZZU has 4 × 71 = 284.
"Label" below means a MIMIC or ZZU label.

1. **ILP-free map (primary).** For each pair, ρ(l, s) is the Spearman correlation across the
   1000 prototypes between label column l and PTB-XL column s, at seed 42. Report the
   min–max over the 5 seeds.
2. **Specificity.** Double-center the dataset's labels × 71 ρ matrix. With only 4 ZZU
   labels, the column means are dominated by those 4, so ZZU specificity is a contrast
   among its own labels.
3. **Candidates**, as in 754f94a: |ρ| ≥ 0.5, or among the label's 3 highest-ρ statements.
4. **Survivors**, as in 754f94a: a candidate that is unexpected or not expected, has ≥ 30
   PTB-XL train positives, has specificity of the same sign as ρ, and keeps the sign of ρ
   at all 5 seeds. Reported in two tiers: unexpected, not expected.
5. **Pigeonhole check of the ILP map**, as in 754f94a: for each seed and label, the 14
   assigned prototypes, their PTB-XL statements, the shared counts (chance ≈ 0.2 per seed),
   row ranks and score percentiles. Pigeonholed = mostly (≥ half, pooled over seeds) at row
   rank > 3 or below the 25th score percentile, or the pair isn't a step-3 candidate.
6. **Random-positives null (new).** As in section 4 of `echonext-ptbxl-crossmap.ipynb`,
   in PTB-XL's projected-prototype space:
   - For each PTB-XL statement with n train positives, draw random sets of n PTB-XL train
     ECGs: 2000 sets for n ≤ 300, 200 for n ≤ 2000, 100 above.
   - Build each set's effect-size column exactly as the ILP does, then take ρ against the
     dataset's label columns. The label columns are built from its train activations and
     labels and held fixed.
   - `p_null` is two-sided on |ρ|. `p_spec_null` uses the specificity matrix rebuilt with
     that column replaced. q values are BH within the dataset.
   - A survivor is **null-supported** if `q_spec_null` < 0.05 and its specificity has the
     same sign in bank and projected space.
   - Reported descriptively: ρ in projected space on the dataset's **test** split (held out
     from the association step), and the label's train positive count. Labels with < 200
     train positives (MIMIC ECMO, mortality during stay, cardiac arrest, inotropes; ZZU
     cardiomyopathy, Kawasaki disease) have noisy columns of their own. The null holds the
     label side fixed, so it doesn't cover them; they are flagged.
7. **Positive controls (new).** The map should recover these, each as a hit among the
   label's top 3 statements by ρ:

   | dataset | label | any of |
   |---|---|---|
   | MIMIC | Atrial fibrillation | AFIB |
   | MIMIC | Atrial flutter | AFLT, AFIB |
   | MIMIC | Myocardial infarction | MI or injury group |
   | MIMIC | Other chronic ischemic heart disease | MI group |
   | MIMIC | Chronic pulmonary heart disease | RV group |
   | MIMIC | Paroxysmal ventricular tachycardia | ventricular ectopy, MI or LV conduction group |
   | MIMIC | Heart valve replaced | PACE, LV conduction group |
   | MIMIC | Heart failure with reduced EF | CLBBB, ILBBB, IVCD, MI group |
   | ZZU | Congenital heart disease | RV group |
   | ZZU | Cardiomyopathy | LVH, ST-T or LV conduction group |

   If most controls fail, the map isn't reading those labels, and their survivors aren't
   interpreted.

## What a survivor means and does not

- A survivor, null-supported or not, is a hypothesis to explain, not a finding.
- MIMIC's labels are visit diagnoses and outcomes. So treatment (digoxin, pacing,
  anticoagulation), comorbidity, and acuity (the "sick patient" axis) are the first
  explanations to rule out, followed by co-labeling within PTB-XL.
- No p-values are reported for ρ itself. Prototypes aren't independent, and the
  associations come from train labels. The step-6 null calibrates only the PTB-XL side.
- A null-supported unexpected or specific not-expected survivor would go on to a
  separately prespecified patient-level screen. For MIMIC that screen can use the
  recorded machine reads as well as the imputed statements, on MIMIC val + test.
